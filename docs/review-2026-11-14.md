## Scope and method

I read every file in `R/`, `scenarios/`, `starter/`, `tests/`, `scripts/`, and
`www/`, plus the CI workflow, Dockerfile, and docs. I ran the engine against
both authored seeds to confirm each suspected defect before changing code, then
drove the app in a headless browser through a full intermediate run.

Overall: the design is sound. Truth, recalled, and collected data stay separate.
Time, missingness, and frozen snapshots behave the way a real investigation
does. Session restore validates its input carefully. The defects below were
specific and fixable. The larger items at the end are design choices for you.

## Open recommendations

These change the lesson or the architecture, so I left them for your decision.
Ranked by value within each group.

### Epidemiology

1. **Give well guests background symptoms.** Symptoms occur only in simulated
   cases, so every clinical definition has specificity 1.0. On the introductory
   seed, sensitivity runs 0.91 (diarrhea only), 0.95 (standard), 1.00 (any
   symptom). The broadest definition is always best, which teaches the wrong
   lesson. Unrelated mild illness in a few percent of attendees, with onsets
   spread over several days, would make both the clinical and the time criteria
   matter.
2. **Add non-response and under-reporting.** Every contacted guest answers and
   every ill guest phones in. A response rate below 100% that differs by
   illness would let you teach selection bias.
3. **Offer differential recall.** Recall error is the same for ill and well
   guests, which biases toward the null on average. An option where cases
   over-report a rumoured food would teach the opposite risk.
4. **Make laboratory results less tidy.** All three stool samples are positive
   and the food test never misses. Imperfect sensitivity and a negative or
   pending result would support the existing message that a negative does not
   clear a food.
5. **Record onset as a date and time.** Guests report onset as "23.7 hours
   after the meal". A clock time would be more natural and would make learners
   compute incubation themselves.
6. **Add a contrasting agent.** A second scenario with a short-incubation toxin
   or norovirus with secondary cases would make incubation and symptom profile
   informative. Today both scenarios share one agent.
7. **Tie action conditions to evidence.** `cluster_confirmed` is always true
   and `ongoing_exposure` always false, so the feedback is fixed. Leftovers
   taken home are a plausible ongoing exposure worth modelling.
8. **Close the menu leak.** Asking one guest what they ate adds every food
   column to the line list, even if Pat was never asked for the menu.
9. **Small cells.** The interval is blank when a cell is zero. An exact or
   score interval, or a plain note, would help.

### Software

1. **Move scenario prose into YAML.** `chicken_salad`, "Lou", the 28-hour
   late-report rule, and the dialogue's locked food names are constants in R.
   A new scenario cannot be added without editing code.
2. **Split `app_server.R`.** It is one 540-line function with lines over 300
   characters, and `style.css` is minified by hand. Shiny modules per step plus
   `styler` and `lintr` in CI would make changes safer to review.
3. **Name the guide steps.** `go_step(4L)`, `go_step(8L)`, "of 9", and the
   draft keys `"2"`, `"4"`, `"8"` break if a step is added.
4. **Merge the two guide validators** in `guide.R` and `persistence.R`.
5. **Treat `ellmer` and `shinychat` as optional.** They are hard imports for an
   optional feature. Moving them to `Suggests` with `skip_if_not_installed()`
   would lighten the default deployment.
6. **Cap text at input.** Restore rejects fields over 20,000 characters, but
   the app accepts them, so an over-long answer makes a session unrestorable.
7. **Cache the renv library in CI** to shorten runs.
8. **Readability.** Several labels are 10 to 12 px, and tables show
   snake_case column names that scroll sideways on a phone.
